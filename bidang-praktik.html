02 — PRACTICE AREAS

Expertise across disciplines.
One standard of service.

KHAI & Partners maintains sixteen areas of practice. Each is treated not as an isolated service line but as a domain requiring its own doctrinal fluency and procedural judgment

a range shaped in part by the industries most active in East Kalimantan, from energy and natural resources to maritime trade.

01

Litigation & Dispute Resolution

Representation before courts of general and specialized jurisdiction, and through mediation and arbitration where a negotiated or adjudicated resolution better serves the client's interest. Litigation strategy, properly understood, begins before a complaint is ever filed — in the evidentiary and doctrinal groundwork that determines whether a claim is sound, and in an honest assessment of what a court is realistically likely to award. We advise clients on that assessment directly, including when the more prudent course is settlement rather than trial, because the objective of representation is the client's interest, not the length of the docket.

02

Criminal Law (Hukum Pidana)

Defense and complainant-side representation in criminal proceedings, from the investigative stage through trial, grounded in the procedural protections of the Indonesian Criminal Procedure Code (KUHAP). We regard the investigative stage — questioning, the gathering of evidence, the determination of suspect status — as the phase in which a case is most often won or lost, and we engage there rather than waiting for an indictment to formalize the matter.

03

Cyber Law & Digital Crime

Counsel on electronic transactions, data protection, and offenses under the ITE Law (Undang-Undang Informasi dan Transaksi Elektronik), including fraud, defamation, and unauthorized access conducted through digital means. As commercial and personal disputes increasingly originate in electronic communication, we treat digital evidence — its authentication, chain of custody, and admissibility — as a technical discipline in its own right, not an afterthought to conventional litigation.

04

Corporate & Commercial Law

Counsel on entity formation, corporate restructuring, mergers and acquisitions, and commercial agreements, grounded in the recognition that a commercial document is tested not at signing but at the moment of dispute — and ought to be drafted with that eventual scrutiny in mind rather than as a formality to be completed quickly.

05

Banking & Finance Law

Advice on lending arrangements, security and collateral structures, and regulatory compliance for financial transactions, with particular attention to the perfection of security interests — a step routinely underestimated by borrowers and lenders alike, and one on which the practical value of a facility agreement often turns.

06

Capital Markets & Securities

Counsel on securities regulation and compliance for enterprises accessing capital markets, including the disclosure and governance obligations that attach once a company looks beyond private financing toward public or semi-public capital.

07

Bankruptcy & Suspension of Debt Payment Obligations (PKPU)

Representation through insolvency proceedings, debt restructuring, and the orderly settlement of assets under Indonesia's bankruptcy and PKPU framework, in which creditor and debtor interests must be reconciled within strict statutory timelines. We advise both creditors seeking recovery and debtors seeking a viable restructuring path, recognizing that the two positions require genuinely different strategic postures rather than a single template applied to both.

08

Employment Law

Counsel on industrial relations, labor disputes, and internal policy instruments, informed by the structural protections Indonesian labor law affords to workers — protections an employer must navigate with precision rather than assumption, and which a worker is entitled to have enforced rather than merely recited.

09

Property & Land Law

Support for property transactions, licensing, land disputes, and development projects, in a jurisdiction where a layered land-registration regime — in which customary and formally certified title can coexist uneasily on the same parcel — rewards precision and penalizes shortcuts taken at the due-diligence stage.

10

Natural Resources, Mining & Energy Law

Advice on licensing, contractual, and regulatory matters in the mining and oil-and-gas sectors that anchor East Kalimantan's economy, an area of practice in which the relevant law is rarely found in a single statute but assembled from overlapping national and regional regulation that changes with some regularity.

11

Maritime & Shipping Law

Counsel on carriage of goods, port operations, and maritime disputes arising from Balikpapan's role as a shipping and logistics hub, where cargo, charterparty, and port-liability questions carry commercial stakes disproportionate to the size of the underlying paperwork.

12

Administrative & Regulatory Law

Representation before government agencies and licensing authorities, and in disputes concerning state administrative decisions — the branch of practice in which the citizen or enterprise meets the machinery of the state directly, and in which procedural rights against an unfavorable administrative act are frequently underused simply because they are underknown.

13

Tax Law

Advice on tax compliance and representation in disputes with tax authorities, an area we approach with particular care for advocates specifically, given that Indonesian advocate services fall outside the simplified UMKM tax regime and are instead subject to standard progressive treatment — a distinction that surprises many practitioners and their clients alike.

14

Civil Law

Handling of civil disputes, breach of contract, unlawful acts (perbuatan melawan hukum), and contractual controversies — the doctrinal core from which much of Indonesian private-law practice descends, and the area in which the precision of a pleading's factual narrative often matters as much as the legal theory attached to it.

15

Family & Inheritance Law

Counsel on marriage, divorce, custody, and inheritance matters under Indonesian civil and religious-court frameworks, handled with the discretion such matters require and a recognition that the legal outcome is rarely separable, for the client, from its personal and familial consequences.

16

Intellectual Property Law

Protection of trademarks, copyright, patents, and other intangible assets whose value, increasingly, exceeds that of the tangible enterprise built around them — assets that are only as defensible as the diligence with which they were registered and maintained.
